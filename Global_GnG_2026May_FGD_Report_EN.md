# May 2026 GnG Three-Market FGD Research Report

## 1. Project Background

This report is based on the May 2026 Gold & Glory / GnG core combat FGD materials across Mexico, Vietnam, and Indonesia. It focuses on the new 3C combat experience: camera view, controls, dodge/mobility, lock-on/auto-aim, block/parry, class combat roles, UI, and combat information. PvE, art/theme, audio, social play, and new character concepts are treated as supplementary observations.

- **New players**: VN Group 1, ID Group 3 New User, and first-touch feedback from MX shooter-reference players. These materials are used to observe the appeal of the new 3C combat, onboarding friction, and information comprehension cost.
- **Hardcore veteran players**: MX KOL/veteran groups, VN Group 3, and ID Group 1 Hardcore. These materials are used to assess whether legacy feel, skill ceiling, dungeon pressure, class weaknesses, and build depth are preserved.
- **Churned veteran players**: VN Group 2 veteran/churned players, ID Group 2 Churned, and MX veteran return signals. These materials are used to assess whether the new version can bring players back and whether key churn blockers have been addressed.

The three markets have different material depth. MX is mainly based on English summary notes, VN includes summaries, surveys, and transcripts, and ID includes a master sheet, PDF summary, and transcripts. To keep the comparison consistent, this report prioritizes materials that have already been structured into findings, while keeping sources in the evidence table.

## 2. Executive Conclusions

The new GnG 3C experience has clear appeal for new users. Players recognize the dark fantasy / medieval theme, 3v3 team cooperation, class roles, skill presentation, and parts of the hit feedback. New players are more easily attracted by "good-looking, easy to try, playable with friends"; veteran and core players care more about whether dungeon tension, skill ceiling, class weaknesses, and gear/build depth from the old version still exist.

New players are mainly blocked by "hard to control and inaccurate lock-on"; veteran players mainly worry that strong assists and an overly wide camera weaken the skill ceiling, dungeon tension, and class weaknesses.

The split between new and veteran players is the second core risk. New players need clearer information, easier inputs, and a readable information hierarchy. Veteran players need less forced assistance, more room for skill expression, more stable class differences, and stronger dungeon pressure.

The appeal of combat itself has been validated, especially the new 3C, 3v3 cooperation, skill actions, and class roles. However, the full loot-fight-extract loop has not yet been validated under the new 3C framework. It should be tested through the internal tournament after FGD-driven adjustments, and then further validated with veteran players.

## 3. Key Findings

### 3.1 Good: New 3C combat attracts new users; unverified: whether the full loot-fight-extract loop works

**Pros:** Players can identify GnG's appeal. New players are interested in the theme, character appearance, team cooperation, and skill actions. Multiple surveys and discussions also show that players are willing to download, learn about, or keep trying the new version after launch. Compared with 1v1, 3v3 amplifies class roles and team strategy, making it easier to create memorable moments.

**Cons / Risks:** The more validated point is the appeal of "fighting", not the full loot-fight-extract loop. Veteran players' positive feedback is also more conditional: they recognize improvements in new skills, visuals, audio, and parts of hit feedback, but their willingness to continue playing will be affected by camera, class balance, and control agency. The loot-fight-extract loop needs continued validation in the internal tournament after FGD-driven adjustments and in follow-up veteran tests.

### 3.2 Good: Wider camera improves understanding; bad: hardcore veterans feel less pressure and weaker legacy feel

**Pros:** New players are more likely to accept the wider camera because it lets them see more of the environment, enemies, and teammates, lowering first-time comprehension cost. Churned veteran players are also more open to a wider view because it helps them read the fight, find loot, and understand team positioning.

**Cons / Risks:** Hardcore veteran players, KOLs, and some competitive players worry that the camera is too far, making characters smaller and reducing pressure, ambush surprise, and dungeon tension. Camera design is not simply "the wider the better". It needs to preserve close-range pressure, legacy responsiveness, and readability in narrow map spaces.

### 3.3 Good: Lighter controls are recognized; bad: button load, input delay, and mis-taps are blocking new players

**Pros:** The lighter, faster, more action-oriented control direction is broadly recognized. It lowers the mental barrier for new players during trial play and makes churned players feel that the new version is more flexible than the old one.

**Cons / Risks:** Control issues concentrate on whether inputs feel reliable. Players repeatedly mention attack delay, skills requiring multiple taps, buttons that are too small or too close, HUD adjustment needs, low roll/dash scores, awkward walk-run transitions, and unnatural casting rules. These issues directly weaken the perceived fairness of PvP.

### 3.4 Good: Dodge and mobility make combat more action-oriented; bad: ranged classes may become too safe

**Pros:** Dodge, roll, and dash are positive directions. Players like their value for chasing, escaping, and avoiding skills. Veteran players also recognize that they make combat more action-oriented.

**Cons / Risks:** The current version still has three issues: distance and cooldown are hard to understand, button/trigger cost is high, and mobility value is not clearly differentiated by class. New players want roll/dash to be easy to press, clear in distance, and clear in feedback. Veteran players care more about whether mobility makes ranged classes too safe or dilutes the value of melee engage.

### 3.5 Good: Auto lock-on can lower the entry barrier; bad: wrong lock-on removes target choice

**Pros:** Lock-on and auto-aim help some new players. They can reduce the initial pressure of action combat controls and help players understand "who should I attack".

**Cons / Risks:** The real question is not simply whether auto lock-on is on or off, but whether the lock-on logic matches player intent. Players are frustrated by accidental monster lock, random enemy lock, the camera being pulled away by the system, and situations where they still need to aim manually after lock-on. Different classes and different skills need different lock-on rules, not one universal auto-lock rule.

### 3.6 Good: Block and parry add combat depth; bad: universal block weakens class and weapon identity

**Pros:** Block, parry, and counter still matter as core assets of GnG combat depth. Veteran players treat parry and counterattack as high-risk, high-reward moments, and new players can also understand the value of defensive actions.

**Cons / Risks:** The risk is that "universal block" flattens class and weapon identity. If staffs, books, shields, and weapons can all block in similar ways, ambush value, melee engage, and class weaknesses are weakened. The product should make the defensive rules for Warrior, Wizard, and Oracle distinct, and make successful block timing, audio, visuals, and counter feedback clearer.

### 3.7 Good: Class roles can drive 3v3; bad: class strength and ease of use are unstable

**Pros:** Class difference is central to 3v3 appeal. Players can feel the role split between Warrior / Wizard / Oracle or Knight / Mage / Healer, and they like 3v3 more because of team cooperation and class responsibility.

**Cons / Risks:** The biggest current conflict is unstable class strength and class experience. Warrior is usually easiest to understand, but may feel ordinary or lack damage and hit certainty. Wizard / Mage may feel hard for new players because of aiming, loading, and casting rhythm, but may feel too strong for veteran players because range, control, dash, and block stack together. Oracle / Priest is appealing because of healing, control, and appearance, but may also risk feeling too complete.

### 3.8 Good: Information layering can serve both new and veteran players; bad: current combat information still confuses new players

**Pros:** Combat information can be layered by player stage. New players need clearer HP, enemy/ally distinction, monsters, Boss, teammate state, skill labels, target prompts, and map interactions. Veteran players can retain more uncertainty and dungeon pressure in high-pressure modes.

**Cons / Risks:** Enemy/ally recognition and target states are still not clear enough in 3v3. Players need to quickly understand who is a teammate, who is an enemy, who is down, who can be revived, how monster HP differs from player HP, and what the current target and win condition are.

### 3.9 Good: Art and audio improve atmosphere; bad: dark visuals and audio clutter hurt combat judgment

**Pros:** Theme and sound are strengths. Players recognize the fantasy / medieval / dungeon atmosphere, like the music, skill sounds, and parts of vibration feedback, and can feel improvements in new hit feedback.

**Cons / Risks:** The main problem is hierarchy. In dark maps, characters, monsters, terrain, and interaction buttons easily blend together. When multiple players cast skills, sound becomes dense, and enemy skills, ally skills, footstep direction, distance, and hit source are not clear enough. Art and audio are not only presentation issues; they affect combat information judgment.

### 3.10 Good: PvE has cooperation and loot fantasy appeal; bad: rule comprehension and replay depth are insufficient

**Pros:** New players like cooperative dungeons and loot fantasy. PvE can be an entry point for understanding the world, class cooperation, and reward loop.

**Cons / Risks:** The main PvE issue is not whether the theme is appealing, but whether players first understand how to play and whether they find it fun after understanding it. New players often do not understand goals, routes, Maid / safe zone / checkpoint, event feedback, and failure reasons. Veteran players can roughly understand the basic rules, but feel that the gameplay is simple, repetitive, low in fun, and weak in replay motivation.
